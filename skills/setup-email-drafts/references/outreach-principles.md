# Cold Outreach Principles

Follow these when writing every email. Based on Hormozi's value-first framework and proven B2B cold email patterns.

---

## The Hormozi Framework — Core Rules

**Rule 0: You already built something for them. Act like it.**
This is NOT a pitch email. You built a working demo of THEIR chatbot. Lead from that position of power. Don't ask permission. Don't introduce yourself. Tell them what you did.

**Rule 1: Dream Outcome First.**
Open with the outcome they want, not the mechanism you built. Don't: "Wir haben einen KI-Chatbot für Sie gebaut." Do: "Wir haben Ihren Kundenservice auf Autopilot gestellt." The first describes a tool. The second describes their life after.

**Rule 2: Specificity = Credibility.**
Vague claims are ignored. Specific claims are believed. Not "wir haben Ihre Produkte eingebaut" → "wir haben Ihre 3 Servicepakete, die Preisliste und die häufigsten Fragen eingebaut." Name something only someone who actually read their website would know. A product name. A specific service. A line from their About page.

**Rule 3: Lower the friction to zero — but never claim it's free.**
They don't need to register, commit, or set anything up. You built it already. It's live. They just need to look. One click. Frame it as zero effort, NOT zero cost — "kostenlos" implies the product is free, which it isn't, and creates distrust when they find out. Also avoid "kein Vertrag nötig" — it sounds like you're already expecting pushback.

Good: "fertig, direkt testbar", "ein Klick, keine Anmeldung", "läuft bereits — einfach reinschauen", "keine Registrierung, direkt ansehen"
Bad: "kostenlos", "kein Vertrag nötig", "unverbindlich"

**Rule 4: Make the value feel like theft.**
If they scroll past this email, they're leaving money on the table. Don't beg. Frame it as: "I did the work. Here it is. You'd be crazy not to look." Hormozi calls this the Grand Slam Offer — the value is so obvious that saying no feels stupid.

**Rule 5: The highlight box = their exact workflow, automated.**
Don't describe InboxMate features. Describe THEIR daily grind, eliminated. If they run a storage facility: "Verfügbarkeit prüfen, Einheiten reservieren, Preise erklären — ohne dass Sie ans Telefon müssen." Every word must be about their operation. Zero generic.

**Rule 6: Curiosity gap subject line.**
The subject must make them think "what did they build for us?" — NOT explain what it is. "[Company] — haben wir was für euch" beats "[Company] — KI-Chatbot Demo". Hide the punchline. They open to find out.

**Rule 7: Short wins. Every sentence must earn it.**
Max 3-4 short paragraphs. If a sentence doesn't increase their desire to click the demo, delete it. No "wir freuen uns", no "in heutiger Zeit", no "als führendes Unternehmen". Kill every filler word.

**Rule 8: One CTA. The demo.**
No call request. No "lassen Sie uns sprechen." Just: click the demo. That's all. Everything else is noise.

**Rule 9: The "Name Swap" Test.**
After writing, ask yourself: could you send this exact email to a different company just by swapping the name? If yes → the email is too generic. Find something that only applies to THIS company and weave it in. This especially applies to subject lines — generic patterns like "[Name], eine Idee für [domain]" or "[Name], das könnte zu [Company] passen" fail at 3x the rate of problem-specific subjects.

**Rule 10: No first names in subject lines.**
Data from 350+ sent emails: subjects starting with a first name ("Andreas, ...", "Florian, ...") pattern-match to spam/sales templates in DACH markets. Engaged emails use first names in subjects only 19% of the time vs 56% in non-clicked emails. Lead with the company name, a business problem, or a curiosity gap instead.

**Rule 11: Always Sie for cold outreach.**
94% of emails that led to chatbot engagement used Sie-Form. Du-Form feels presumptuous from a stranger in B2B DACH. Only use du if: (1) prior communication already established du, OR (2) the company website prominently uses du throughout AND it's a creative/tech/startup. When in doubt → Sie.

**Rule 12: Question subjects outperform statements.**
Subjects that ask a pain-point question convert at 2.4x the rate of statement subjects. The question must be specific to their business — not generic like "Wer berät Ihre Kunden?" but pointed like "4 Standorte, 91% Weiterempfehlung, 0 Antworten um 21 Uhr?" or "Wer nimmt Transportanfragen nachts entgegen?"

## Deliverability — Spam-Filter Blocklist (Rule 19, applies to subject AND body AND follow-ups)

A perfect email that lands in spam converts at exactly 0%. Content is one of three spam signals (besides DNS/auth and sender reputation) and the only one the copy controls. HARD bans:

**Never in the SUBJECT:**
- Discounts/prices of any kind: "Rabatt", "%", "€", "sparen", "Angebot", "Deal"
- Urgency: "Letzte Chance", "nur noch", "nur heute", "läuft ab", "jetzt"
- Free-bait: "gratis", "kostenlos", "free", "geschenkt"
- "!"/"?" stacking, ALL-CAPS words, emoji
- Spam classics: "exklusiv", "garantiert", "Gewinn", "limitiert"

**Never in the BODY:**
- Percentage discounts ("50% Rabatt", "50% off") — write "Sonderkonditionen"/"special pricing" if a deal must be mentioned at all
- "gratis"/"kostenlos"/"keine Kreditkarte" (also violates Rule 3) — frame trials as "der Test endet von selbst — nichts zu kündigen"
- Urgency phrases ("Letzte Chance", "nur noch X Tage") — the countdown lives on the DEMO PAGE, not in the email
- More than ONE link besides the footer (the CTA button is the one link; never add tracking pixels or link shorteners)
- Link text that differs from the link target's domain (e.g. text "google.com" pointing elsewhere)
- Attachments of any kind

**Always:**
- The CTA links to demo.inboxmate.psquared.dev (established sending pattern) — never bit.ly/t.co/etc.
- Footer keeps the full imprint (psquared GmbH · Dametzstraße 2-4 · 4020 Linz, Austria) + working unsubscribe — the template provides both, never strip them
- Plain, personal business German. The more an email reads like one human writing to another (and the less like a campaign), the better it delivers — which is the same thing Rules 2/7/9 demand for conversion.

## Length — Hormozi Cold-Email Format (Rule 20, HARD limits)

Cold emails get skimmed on a phone in 3 seconds. Hormozi's format: lead with THEIR problem, prove you did work for THEM, one ask. Everything else is deleted weight.

- **Outreach body: max 110 words** across greeting + all paragraphs + closing (befundItems bullets count as ~4 words each). If you're over, cut bodyParagraph2 detail first.
- **Follow-up body: max 50 words.** One reminder thought + the link. Never re-pitch.
- Paragraphs are 1-2 sentences. No paragraph over 25 words.
- ONE ask only (the demo link → meeting). No "P.S.", no second CTA.
- Subject: max 7 words.
- The Befund list does the proving — don't repeat its content in prose.

## Checklist Before Submitting

Run this mentally for every email before creating the draft:

- [ ] **Dream outcome** in para 1? (not "we built X" but "your customers get Y instantly")
- [ ] **Specific detail** from their website? (product name / service / pricing / team)
- [ ] **Risk lowered** — is it clear they don't need to do anything to "try" it?
- [ ] **Highlight box** describes their workflow, not InboxMate features?
- [ ] **Subject** hides the punchline (curiosity gap)?
- [ ] **No filler phrases** — no "wir freuen uns", "unser Produkt", "als führendes", "in heutiger Zeit"
- [ ] **Name swap test passed** — this email could NOT go to a different company unchanged?
- [ ] **No first name in subject?** (use company name or pain-point question instead)
- [ ] **Deliverability blocklist clean?** (no Rabatt/%/gratis/kostenlos/Letzte Chance/urgency in subject or body — Rule 19)
- [ ] **Short enough?** (outreach ≤110 words, follow-up ≤50 words, paragraphs ≤2 sentences — Rule 20)
- [ ] **Sie-Form?** (unless prior du-contact or clearly du-culture company)
- [ ] **Subject is a question or specific teaser?** (not "[Name], eine Idee für...")
- [ ] **Under ~150 words** body total?

## Outreach-Only Rules (Rules 13–17)

These apply to **initial outreach emails only**, NOT to follow-ups. Follow-ups have their own rules below.

**Rule 13: Never open outreach with "Ich habe" or "Wir haben".**
Data from Campaign 15: 74% of emails opened with "Ich habe mir [Company] angeschaut und einen KI-Assistenten gebaut" — zero engagement. All 8 engaged emails from earlier campaigns opened with the prospect's problem or industry context. bodyParagraph1 must start with their world, not yours. Bad: "Ich habe für X einen Bot gebaut." Good: "Produktionsfirmen sind oft im Dreh — und potenzielle Kunden warten nicht."

**Rule 14: No filler phrases as substitutes for content.**
"Fertig, direkt testbar — ein Klick, keine Anmeldung" is a zero-friction signal, not a paragraph. It belongs woven into a sentence, not bolted on as standalone filler. If bodyParagraph1 is just "I built a thing, it's ready, click here" — the email has no hook.

**Rule 15: Proper German characters are mandatory (outreach AND follow-up).**
Never use ae/oe/ue/ss instead of ä/ö/ü/ß. 38% of Campaign 15 emails had missing umlauts — this screams auto-generated to any German reader. If the model output lacks umlauts, fix them before submitting. This applies to subject, greeting, body, highlight, closing, and signoff. **This rule applies to both outreach and follow-ups.**

**Rule 16: Every outreach email must be unique — no identical closings across a batch.**
Campaign 15 had 74% identical "Bei Fragen einfach antworten." closings. Vary the closing: "Falls es passt, sprechen wir gern kurz.", "Bin gespannt, was Sie davon halten.", "Einfach auf diese Mail antworten." — each email should feel hand-written.

**Rule 17: Minimum outreach body depth.**
bodyParagraph1 + bodyParagraph2 + bodyParagraph3 must total at least 450 characters. Anything shorter means the email is too thin to convey value. Campaign 15 averaged 402 chars vs 515 in engaged campaigns.

## Follow-Up Rules

Follow-ups are fundamentally different from outreach. The prospect already received the initial email. The goal is a short, natural nudge — not a second pitch.

**F1: Referencing the previous email is expected.**
Opening with "Letzte Woche hatte ich Ihnen gezeigt..." or "Vor einer Woche habe ich Ihnen..." is correct and natural for follow-ups. Rule 13 ("never open with Ich habe") does NOT apply here.

**F2: Keep it short — 1-2 paragraphs max.**
Follow-ups should be 150–300 characters body total. Don't repeat the full pitch. A brief callback to the specific product/service from the outreach + "die Demo steht noch bereit" is enough. Rule 17 (min 450 chars) does NOT apply.

**F3: Reference a specific detail from the outreach.**
Don't write a generic "have you seen our demo?" — reference the exact product, service, or pain point from the outreach. "Der Bot, der Ihre SieMatic-Küchenanfragen beantwortet" > "Der Bot, den wir für Sie erstellt haben".

**F4: Never say "Follow-up", "Nachfrage", "Erinnerung", or "Reminder" in the subject.**
These words scream sales automation. Use curiosity or a continuation of the outreach's thread: "Bauherren im Tegernseer Tal googeln sonntags – antwortet jemand?", "[Company] — Demo läuft noch", "Herr [Name], kurz reingeschaut?"

**F5: Proper umlauts mandatory (same as Rule 15).**
No ae/oe/ue in follow-ups either.

**F6: Match du/Sie consistently with the outreach.**
If the outreach used Sie, the follow-up must use Sie. Never mix.

## Subagent Batch Rules

**Rule 18: Subagent batch instructions must be explicit.**
When delegating email writing to subagents, provide: (1) the exact Hormozi rules by number, (2) examples of winning emails from earlier campaigns, (3) explicit instruction to open outreach with the prospect's pain not "Ich habe", (4) explicit instruction to use proper umlauts, (5) explicit instruction to vary closings. Subagents cannot infer these requirements — they will default to formulaic patterns without exact templates. For follow-ups: explicitly state that they should be short, reference a specific detail from the outreach, and follow rules F1–F6.

## Legacy Rules (still apply)

**1. Lead with value, not a pitch.**
Open with something you DID for them. Not "our product does." — "We built a chatbot for [Company] that knows your products."

**2. Make it about THEM, not you.**
Every sentence about their business, customers, problems. InboxMate features → rewrite as their benefit.

**3. Show, don't tell.**
The demo IS the pitch. Make them curious enough to click.

**4. Keep it short.**
3-5 short paragraphs. Every sentence earns its place.

**5. One clear CTA.**
The demo button. That's it.

**6. Subject line = curiosity gap.**
Don't give away the punchline.
